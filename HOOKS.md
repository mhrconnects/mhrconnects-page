# Hooks & retention playbook: The AI Classroom

Every script (long video or clip) must pass this before it's rendered.

## 1. The hook (first 1–3 seconds)
**Most viewers decide in the first 2–3 seconds.** The hook works on three channels at once, and must work **muted**:
- **Visual:** something striking on screen in frame 1 (our kinetic title slam + shockwave). No logo, no slow intro.
- **On-screen text:** the hook title, 3–6 words, one *highlighted* word.
- **Voice:** the first sentence is the hook itself. Never "Hey guys" or "In this video".

**How hooks work:** they open a gap the viewer needs to close, and the video must pay it off.
Use one of these mechanisms (write 5 candidates across different ones, keep the best):

| Mechanism | Example for us |
|---|---|
| Curiosity gap | "AI doesn't read words. It reads something smaller." |
| Contrarian | "Stop asking AI questions. Do this instead." |
| Mistake | "Most people use AI wrong in their very first chat." |
| Stakes | "Before you trust AI with anything important, watch this." |
| Specificity | "One sentence that makes Claude's answers twice as useful." *(only if true)* |
| Identity | "If you've never used AI, start here." |
| Question | "Why does AI make things up with total confidence?" |
| Transformation | "Same AI, same question, completely different answer." |

**Score each hook (pass/fail):** Would a stranger need the next second? Does the video actually deliver it?
Is it specific? Does it fit in about 2 seconds of speech (8–12 words)? If not, cut it.

**Banned:** "you won't believe", fake numbers, length claims that aren't true, clickbait the video doesn't pay off.

## 2. Keep them watching
- **Bridge line** right after the hook: carry the curiosity into the body ("Here's what's really going on.").
- **Open loops:** promise something later ("the third one is the one nobody does").
- **Visible progress:** number things ("Power prompt number 3"), use chapters on long videos.
- **Visual change every 2–4 seconds** in clips (a new card, item or highlight). Long videos: re-hook every 60–90 seconds.
- **Cut anything that doesn't grab, teach or move forward.**
- **Captions from word one** (we do this): about 80% watch on mute.

## 3. Pay it off
The payoff must deliver exactly what the hook promised, with the best point last.

## 4. End
- **Clips:** one short call to action (about 2 seconds): "Follow for daily AI lessons." Where possible, make the last line loop
  back to the first so replays feel natural (replays boost reach).
- **Long videos:** a recap, then one call to action plus "next lesson: ___" (the end screen points to it).

## 5. Packaging (YouTube)
Title + thumbnail work together: specific, outcome-focused, curiosity with a clear payoff, readable on a phone.
If the packaging isn't compelling, the idea isn't ready.

## 6. Fix with numbers (YouTube Studio)
| Symptom | Number | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Not clicked | CTR low | Title/thumbnail |
| Clicked then gone | 30-second retention low | Hook doesn't match title, or slow opening |
| Holds then sags | Avg % viewed low | Cut padding, add re-hooks |
| Swiped (Shorts) | "Viewed vs swiped" < 70% | First 2 seconds |

## Lessons from batch 1 (Ep 1 clips)
- Good: hooks lead with a mistake or stakes ("Most people use AI wrong…", "Before you trust AI…").
- Improve next time: shorter scenes in clips (some scenes hold 8–10s; target ≤ 4s per visual change);
  try a loop ending; make 2–3 hook variants per clip to test across platforms.
